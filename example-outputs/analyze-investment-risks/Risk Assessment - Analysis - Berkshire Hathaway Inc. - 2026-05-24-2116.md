# Berkshire Hathaway Inc. - Risk Analysis

**Date:** 2026-05-24  
**Ticker:** BRK-B

---

## Executive Summary

Berkshire Hathaway's risk profile is **MODERATE** rather than low because the balance sheet is unusually strong but the business is not simple. The main risks are not near-term solvency or a single customer problem. They are insurance severity, PacifiCorp wildfire litigation and regulatory recovery, post-Buffett capital allocation, GEICO competitive/service durability, equity-market exposure, and the opportunity cost of holding hundreds of billions of dollars in cash and Treasury Bills.

The risk profile is cushioned by $373.5B of cash, equivalents and U.S. Treasury Bills at March 31, 2026 in insurance and other businesses, $727.2B of shareholders' equity, negative-cost float, and decentralized operating businesses. The risk that matters is whether those advantages continue compounding after the Buffett-to-Abel transition while BHE, GEICO, BNSF and the equity portfolio face segment-specific pressures.

**Overall Risk Level:** MODERATE

---

## Risk Assessment by Category

### 1. Business Model Risks

Berkshire is an insurance-led holding company, not a normal industrial company. That structure reduces single-business fragility but creates complexity. Investors must understand insurance float, equity marks, regulated utility liabilities, railroad economics, and dozens of operating subsidiaries at once. Misreading GAAP net income or consolidated revenue can lead to wrong risk conclusions.

The most important business-model risk is **capital deployment at scale**. Berkshire's cash and Treasury Bill balance is now so large that even good subsidiary results can be diluted if capital remains idle for too long. The Q1 2026 10-Q shows insurance and other businesses held $373.5B of cash, equivalents and Treasury Bills net of unsettled purchases. This is a fortress, but also an opportunity-cost problem if acquisitions, equities, or repurchases are not attractive.

The second business-model risk is **succession of judgment**. Greg Abel became CEO on January 1, 2026, and Buffett remains chairman. The mechanics of succession were clean, but Berkshire's most valuable intangible asset is judgment: when not to buy, when to buy aggressively, and how to preserve trust with sellers and subsidiary managers.

### 2. Competitive Risks

Berkshire's parent-level moat is stronger than several of its operating-company moats. GEICO faces intense price and service competition in private passenger auto. The FY2025 annual report says the five largest private passenger auto insurers had about 63.6% share and GEICO was third at about 11.6%. Auto insurance is a switchable product, and Progressive's long-term momentum remains the key competitive comparison.

GEICO's underwriting profitability improved, but Q1 2026 shows the risk is not gone. GEICO's combined ratio rose to 87.3% from 79.8% in Q1 2025, with loss ratio up 4.9 points and expense ratio up 2.6 points. That is still profitable, but it shows growth, advertising, claims inflation and service repair can pressure the economics.

BNSF is protected by physical rail-network scarcity, but it competes with Union Pacific, trucks, intermodal alternatives and customer service expectations. The moat example already capped Berkshire's BNSF/GEICO advantages because parent-level scale does not make every subsidiary best-in-class.

### 3. Financial Risks

Berkshire's balance sheet is a major mitigant, but the company has meaningful financial volatility. Equity securities can create large GAAP swings. The Q1 2026 10-Q reports $1.6B of pre-tax investment losses for the quarter and explicitly says investment gains/losses often have little analytical or predictive value. That is economically sensible, but it still creates headline volatility and can affect book value and statutory perceptions.

Insurance reserves are another financial risk. Berkshire had approximately $176.9B of float at March 31, 2026 and negative-cost float in Q1 2026, but reserves are estimates. Retroactive reinsurance, long-tail casualty, asbestos/environmental exposures, and catastrophe risk can all create adverse development.

BHE and BNSF carry large capital needs and debt at subsidiary levels. Berkshire's parent does not guarantee all subsidiary debt, but value creation depends on regulated returns, capital access, and cost recovery.

### 4. Operational Risks

GEICO's operational rebuild remains a monitoring item. FY2025 results showed policies-in-force growth resumed, but Q1 2026 showed higher claims frequencies, severity, advertising and acquisition-related expenses. The question is whether GEICO can grow while maintaining an attractive combined ratio and improving customer service.

BNSF faces execution risk in service reliability, labor, safety, weather and capex productivity. Q1 2026 was strong, with pre-tax earnings up 13.5% and volumes up 2.2%, but rail economics can deteriorate quickly if service quality falls or labor/fuel inflation outruns pricing.

Decentralization is a strength and a risk. A small headquarters reduces bureaucracy, but cyber, safety, labor, compliance and culture risks are spread across many subsidiaries. The 2026 proxy includes a shareholder proposal criticizing decentralized human-capital oversight, which is a reputational signal even if not a core thesis breaker by itself.

### 5. Regulatory and Political Risks

The largest regulatory/legal risk is PacifiCorp wildfire litigation and cost recovery. The Q1 2026 10-Q reports cumulative probable wildfire losses of about $2.9B through March 31, 2026, $2.3B of settlements paid, $577M of estimated unpaid wildfire liabilities, and no expected additional insurance recoveries. It also says material additional losses beyond accruals are reasonably possible.

Utility regulation matters because BHE's ability to earn on capital depends on allowed returns and recovery of wildfire, infrastructure, fuel and prevention costs. Political backlash after wildfires can limit recovery even when expenditures are necessary.

Insurance regulation is also material. GEICO writes admitted auto policies across all states, with rates regulated by state insurance departments. Regulatory delay in rate approvals can hurt underwriting if loss costs rise faster than premiums.

### 6. Valuation Risks

Berkshire's valuation risk is that investors overpay for safety. The company can be excellent and still produce mediocre forward returns if the market capitalizes cash, equities and operating businesses too generously. Very large cash balances also make it harder for Berkshire to outperform in strong bull markets unless deployment opportunities appear.

The post-Buffett transition adds valuation risk. A lower market multiple on the same assets could occur if investors lose confidence in the Berkshire culture or if Abel's communication style creates uncertainty around buybacks, acquisitions or portfolio moves.

Finally, GAAP net income can lure investors into the wrong multiple. Investment gains/losses can make earnings look unusually high or low, so raw P/E is a weak anchor.

### 7. ESG and Reputational Risks

Berkshire's most material ESG/reputational exposure is BHE wildfire liability and utility safety. Large wildfire settlements or findings of gross negligence can harm both economics and public trust. The PacifiCorp litigation record is already large enough to matter.

Labor and safety risk also matter across BNSF, NetJets, manufacturing, energy and service subsidiaries. A decentralized model can make subsidiary-level issues appear disconnected until a severe incident creates parent-level attention.

Governance risk is unusual. Buffett's long tenure and concentrated ownership created trust, but the post-Buffett era will be judged on board independence, compensation choices, transparency, and capital allocation. The 2026 proxy states Berkshire does not use Berkshire stock in compensating employees; a major drift from that culture would be a reputational warning.

## Top 5 Risks (Ranked by Severity)

1. **PacifiCorp wildfire liabilities exceed accruals (Severity: 9):** Additional wildfire losses or weak cost recovery could reduce BHE value and require more capital support. *Estimated Impact: $0.5B-$3B+ additional pre-tax loss risk at BHE/PacifiCorp level, plus potential multiple discount on regulated utility value.*

2. **Post-Buffett capital allocation underperforms (Severity: 6):** A few poor acquisitions, poor buyback timing, or loss of seller trust would damage Berkshire's core compounding engine. *Estimated Impact: 100-300 bps annual compounding drag and possible 5-15% holding-company multiple compression.*

3. **GEICO loses underwriting edge while regrowing (Severity: 6):** Advertising, claims inflation, service gaps, or Progressive-led competition could push combined ratios higher. *Estimated Impact: 5-15% pressure on insurance operating earnings if GEICO combined ratio normalizes materially above the mid/high-80s.*

4. **Equity-market drawdown hits book value (Severity: 6):** A 25-35% equity-market decline would flow through reported earnings/book value and could pressure sentiment. *Estimated Impact: $70B-$100B mark-to-market hit if the equity portfolio fell roughly 25-35%, partly offset by cash and operating earnings.*

5. **Catastrophe or reserve shock (Severity: 6):** Mega-catastrophe, casualty inflation, or adverse retroactive reinsurance development could create a large underwriting loss. *Estimated Impact: $5B-$15B pre-tax loss in a severe insurance year, with larger tail risk in extreme events.*

## Thesis Killers

The following would warrant immediate sale or a full thesis reset:

1. **Berkshire abandons intrinsic-value repurchase discipline:** Large buybacks occur while cash remains high but valuation is not demonstrably attractive. *Timeframe: Immediate.*

2. **Abel-era acquisition shows promotional synergy math or material overpayment:** A major deal relies on aggressive assumptions rather than Berkshire's historical margin of safety. *Timeframe: 1-2 quarters after deal disclosure.*

3. **BHE wildfire/regulatory losses become parent-level capital impairment:** Additional PacifiCorp/BHE losses or blocked recovery create a multi-year capital drain large enough to change Berkshire's utility thesis. *Timeframe: 1+ year to materialize.*

4. **GEICO combined ratio remains above 100% for multiple quarters while policy growth is weak:** That would imply the insurance flywheel is losing both underwriting discipline and competitive relevance. *Timeframe: 1-2 quarters.*

5. **Berkshire changes compensation/culture toward stock awards or short-term financial targets:** A visible break from owner-oriented incentives would indicate cultural drift. *Timeframe: Immediate to 1+ year depending on disclosure.*

## Downside Scenarios

**Probability Methodology:** Informed judgment using current filing disclosures, prior BuffettStack research, and historical cyclicality of insurance, equity markets, utilities, and recessions. Probabilities represent standalone downside paths over roughly three years, are not mutually exclusive in every detail, and sum to less than 100% so the base case remains implicit.

### Mild Downside

- **Assumptions:** GEICO combined ratio drifts into the low/mid-90s, cash remains above $350B, BNSF/BHE grow slowly, and equity markets are flat to down modestly.
- **Impact:** Consolidated revenue roughly flat to down 3%; operating earnings and operating-earnings-per-share proxy down 5-10%; operating margin pressure of 100-200 bps in key subsidiaries; valuation multiple compresses modestly; illustrative price impact/price target range is 10-20% below prevailing market price, not a recommendation.
- **Probability:** 25% - plausible because Q1 2026 already shows GEICO loss and expense pressure while cash drag remains visible.

### Severe Downside

- **Assumptions:** PacifiCorp incurs additional wildfire losses, GEICO combined ratio approaches or exceeds 100%, equity markets fall 25-35%, and Abel does not deploy capital aggressively into the downturn.
- **Impact:** Operating earnings and operating-earnings-per-share proxy down 15-25%; book value hit of $70B-$100B from equities before offsets; utility value impaired by additional litigation/regulatory discount; valuation multiple compresses 10-20%; illustrative price impact/price target range is 25-40% below prevailing market price.
- **Probability:** 15% - requires several major risks to cluster, but each component has a live factual basis.

### Catastrophic Downside

- **Assumptions:** Mega-catastrophe or reserve shock creates a $15B+ insurance loss, BHE faces multi-billion additional wildfire/regulatory losses, equity markets fall 40%+, and the Abel transition loses investor confidence after a poor capital allocation decision.
- **Impact:** Operating earnings and operating-earnings-per-share proxy down 30%+ in stress year; book value materially impaired; severe multiple compression; possible need to slow buybacks/deployment despite attractive prices; illustrative price impact/price target range is 45-60% below prevailing market price.
- **Probability:** 7% - low probability because Berkshire's liquidity is exceptional, but not below 5% given insurance, equity and utility tail exposures.

**Scenario probability total:** 47%, leaving 53% for base/upside cases not shown.
