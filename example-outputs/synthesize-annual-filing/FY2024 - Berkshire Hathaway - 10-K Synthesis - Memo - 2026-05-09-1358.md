# Berkshire Hathaway - FY2024 10-K Synthesis

## Key Takeaways

- **Operating earnings, not GAAP net income, carried the year:** Revenue rose +1.9% to $371.4 billion and operating earnings rose +27.0% to $47.4 billion, while GAAP net income fell -7.5% because investment gains were lower than 2023. The economic engine improved even though mark-to-market results made the headline less clean. `[OPERATING-EARNINGS]`

- **Insurance was the center of gravity:** Underwriting earnings increased to $9.0 billion after tax, float reached about $171 billion, and Berkshire's U.S. insurance statutory surplus was about $310 billion. GEICO was the standout, with an 81.5% combined ratio versus 90.7% in 2023. `[INSURANCE-FLOAT]`

- **Cash optionality became enormous:** Cash, cash equivalents and U.S. Treasury Bills were $334.2 billion at year-end against $124.8 billion of consolidated notes and borrowings. This leaves Berkshire with a net cash-like position while still owning $271.6 billion of public equities and a large base of controlled operating businesses. `[LIQUIDITY]`

- **The portfolio is more resilient than the revenue mix suggests:** Segment revenue is scattered across insurance, manufacturing, distribution, retail, railroad and energy, but earnings power is more concentrated in insurance underwriting, investment income, BNSF, BHE and large operating subsidiaries. The structure is a collection of capital allocation platforms, not a normal conglomerate. `[SEGMENT-MIX]`

- **BNSF and BHE improved but remain watch items:** BNSF net earnings slipped -1.1% despite higher volumes, and BHE earnings recovered as wildfire accruals eased. Both businesses require sustained capital investment and regulatory access to cost recovery. `[REGULATED-ASSETS]`

- **Succession risk is acknowledged, not ignored:** The filing names Greg Abel as the board's current CEO successor if replacement were needed, and Buffett's shareholder letter says Abel will eventually write the annual letters. The question is less "is there a plan?" and more "how much of Berkshire's culture transfers after Buffett?" `[KEY-PERSON]`

## Business Summary

Berkshire Hathaway is a decentralized holding company that owns insurance and reinsurance operations, BNSF Railway, Berkshire Hathaway Energy, and a broad set of manufacturing, service, retailing, distribution and leasing businesses. The operating model is unusual: subsidiary managers run day-to-day businesses with limited centralized functions, while headquarters controls major capital allocation, investments and CEO selection for operating units.

The insurance group is the capital engine. GEICO, Berkshire Hathaway Primary Group and Berkshire Hathaway Reinsurance Group write primary insurance and reinsurance, generate float, and hold large investment portfolios. Float was about $171 billion at the end of 2024, and Berkshire reports that the average cost of float was negative in both 2024 and 2023. This is the key Berkshire flywheel: disciplined underwriting plus large, low-cost policyholder funds invested with a long horizon.

Outside insurance, Berkshire owns asset-heavy regulated businesses in rail and utilities, as well as a collection of operating businesses that span industrial products, building products, consumer products, distribution, auto retailing, fuel retailing and services. The company had about 392,400 employees at year-end, 80% in the U.S. and 20% represented by unions.

## What Changed This Year

- Operating earnings rose to $47.4 billion from $37.4 billion, driven by insurance underwriting, insurance investment income and BHE improvement.
- GEICO's underwriting turnaround accelerated: pre-tax underwriting earnings were $7.8 billion, helped by higher average premiums, lower claim frequencies and operating efficiency.
- Cash and Treasury Bills expanded materially as equity securities declined from $353.8 billion to $271.6 billion and short-term investments in U.S. Treasury Bills rose to $286.5 billion.
- Berkshire acquired the remaining minority interest in BHE late in 2024 for roughly $3.9 billion, including $2.9 billion in cash and the balance in Class B shares.
- BHE earnings improved as PacifiCorp wildfire accrual pressure was lower than 2023, but wildfire litigation and rate recovery remain thesis-critical.
- Repurchases slowed to $2.9 billion versus $9.2 billion in 2023; there is no fixed dollar authorization or expiration date.
- No material accounting red flags were identified; Deloitte issued an unqualified opinion and internal control opinion.

## Management Commentary

> "Berkshire did better than I expected" (p.4)

**Key points from management:**

- Buffett emphasized operating earnings of $47.4 billion rather than GAAP net income because investment gains and losses create large period-to-period volatility.
- The letter credits GEICO's 2024 improvement to Todd Combs, while noting the repair is "not yet complete" (p.4).
- Management continues to prefer ownership of businesses and equities over cash equivalents, but only when opportunities are compelling.
- Berkshire's insurance culture remains explicitly price-disciplined: "never write inadequately-priced policies" (p.9).
- The tone is candid and conservative: strong 2024 results, large liquidity, and a clear warning that insurance mega-losses will eventually occur.

## Risk Factors

Unable to assess [NEW] vs. prior year - single-year analysis.

- **Key-person succession:** The filing says major investment and capital allocation decisions are made by Warren Buffett, age 94, while naming Greg Abel as the board's current successor if replacement were needed (p.K-25). *Probability: High. Impact: Med.* This is not a no-plan risk; it is a culture-transfer and capital-allocation-judgment risk. `[KEY-PERSON]`

- **Equity concentration:** Berkshire says a high percentage of insurance subsidiary equity securities is concentrated in relatively few issuers, and about 71% of equity securities fair value was in five companies at year-end (p.K-25, p.K-62). *Probability: Med. Impact: Med.* The risk is real, but the cash position and statutory surplus reduce forced-sale risk. `[EQUITY-CONCENTRATION]`

- **Insurance catastrophe and reserve risk:** Berkshire is willing to assume more risk from a single event than any other insurer and tries to avoid single-event pre-tax losses above $15 billion (p.K-26). *Probability: Med. Impact: High.* This is core to the moat and the risk: Berkshire can earn superior float economics because it accepts losses others cannot. `[INSURANCE-CATASTROPHE]`

- **Climate, wildfire and regulated cost recovery:** The filing highlights climate events, BHE emissions exposure, wildfire litigation and the need for regulated cost recovery (p.K-26 to p.K-27). *Probability: High. Impact: Med.* This is especially material for BHE and P/C insurance; adverse legal or regulatory outcomes can turn capital intensity into value leakage. `[CLIMATE-WILDFIRE]`

- **Cyber and operational resilience:** Berkshire relies on technology across many decentralized businesses and warns that disruption or intrusion at significant operations could affect results, financial condition or liquidity (p.K-24 to p.K-25). *Probability: Med. Impact: Med.* Decentralization limits single-system exposure but also broadens the attack surface. `[CYBER-OPERATIONAL]`

## Questions for Further Research

1. **How sustainable is GEICO's underwriting improvement once policy count resumes growth?**
   *Why it matters:* The 2024 result benefited from pricing, efficiency and lower frequency; the investment question is whether GEICO can grow without giving back margin.

2. **How should BHE wildfire liabilities and regulatory cost recovery be capitalized in intrinsic value?**
   *Why it matters:* BHE's value depends on allowed returns, liability outcomes and whether regulators permit recovery of investment and wildfire costs.

3. **What is the normalized cash conversion of Berkshire after adjusting for Treasury Bill accretion, insurance working capital and equity sales?**
   *Why it matters:* Reported OCF fell sharply, but Berkshire's cash flow statement is noisy because insurance and investment activity are intertwined.

4. **What is the post-Buffett capital allocation process in practice?**
   *Why it matters:* Greg Abel's operating capability is well signaled; the harder question is how portfolio decisions, buybacks and acquisitions will be governed.

5. **How much of Berkshire's equity portfolio concentration risk is offset by excess statutory surplus and parent liquidity?**
   *Why it matters:* A large equity drawdown would hit GAAP equity and insurance statutory surplus, but the investment conclusion depends on whether it impairs underwriting capacity.
