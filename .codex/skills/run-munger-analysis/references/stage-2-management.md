## Instructions

You are conducting Stage 2 of a 4-stage Munger Analysis. Your role is to assess management quality and validate/challenge Stage 1's financial findings against management's track record.

### Documents to Read

1. **Management Credibility Audit** — `**/[Company]/2-Analysis/2.4-Management-Audit/*.md`

### Critical First Step: Validate Stage 1 Flags

Before assessing management, you MUST address each flag from Stage 1's "Cross-Reference Flags for Stage 2" section.

For each flag, determine:
- **✓ Validated:** Management track record supports Stage 1 finding
- **✗ Challenged:** Management track record contradicts or complicates Stage 1 finding

If challenged, note the tension. This may affect scoring in Stage 4.

### What to Assess

#### 1. Quality of Management (Full Assessment)

Evaluate:
- **Incentive Alignment:** Insider ownership, compensation structure, skin in the game
- **Capital Allocation:** M&A discipline, buyback timing, reinvestment returns, dividend policy
- **Credibility:** Promise delivery rate from audit, Say/Do quadrant placement
- **Candor:** Transparency about mistakes and challenges
- **Focus:** Empire building vs. disciplined focus

**Score:** -2 to +2
**Assessment:** EXCEPTIONAL / GOOD / ADEQUATE / CONCERNING / POOR

**Remember the +2 Gate:** EXCEPTIONAL requires no material concerns. Succession risk, any credibility misses, or compensation concerns typically cap at +1.

#### 2. Predictability (Refinement — Add Execution)

Build on Stage 1's financial stability/volatility factors:
- Does management's track record enhance or reduce predictability?
- Is guidance typically conservative (enhances predictability) or optimistic (reduces it)?
- Does execution consistency match financial stability?

Update the preliminary predictability assessment.

#### 3. Long-Term Orientation (Partial — Management Horizon)

Assess management's time horizon:
- Evidence of long-term thinking (multi-year investments, patient capital allocation)
- Evidence of short-term pressures (quarterly guidance obsession, frequent strategy shifts)

**Preliminary Assessment:** LONG-TERM / MEDIUM-TERM / SHORT-TERM

**Note:** This is partial. Stage 3 will add secular trends and competitive dynamics.

### Cross-Reference Flags

Identify 2-4 items that Stage 3 (Competitive Position) should validate or challenge:

## Scoring Reference

### Quality of Management Scoring

| Score | Assessment | Criteria |
|-------|------------|----------|
| +2 | EXCEPTIONAL | Aligned incentives, excellent capital allocation, high credibility (>85%), candid, focused. NO concerns. |
| +1 | GOOD | Strong management, but has succession risk OR some credibility gaps OR compensation concerns |
| 0 | ADEQUATE | Competent but unremarkable. Neither helps nor hurts thesis |
| -1 | CONCERNING | Misaligned incentives, poor capital allocation, or low credibility (<60%) |
| -2 | POOR | Multiple red flags. Trust issues. Potential thesis killer |

### Say/Do Quadrant Reference

| Quadrant | Characteristics | Investment Implication |
|----------|-----------------|------------------------|
| Credible Consistent | Specific guidance + delivers | High trust in management claims |
| Underpromise Overdeliver | Conservative + beats | Positive surprises likely |
| Overpromise Underdeliver | Specific + misses | Discount all guidance |
| Empty Suits | Vague + unreliable | Avoid entirely |

## Key Principles

1. **Validate, don't assume:** Explicitly address each Stage 1 flag with evidence
2. **Tensions are valuable:** If Stage 1 financials look great but management credibility is poor, that's important information
3. **Use the audit data:** Reference specific credibility percentages, not just impressions
4. **Capital allocation is key:** This is Munger's primary management lens — how do they deploy capital?
5. **Preserve evidence:** Quote key credibility findings for Stage 4 to use
6. **Flag for Stage 3:** Management often makes moat claims — flag these for external validation
