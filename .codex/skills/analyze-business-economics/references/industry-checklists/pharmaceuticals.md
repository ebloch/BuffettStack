## Required Metrics (Section 4)

### Revenue Metrics (ALL REQUIRED)

| Metric | Benchmark | Why It Matters |
|--------|-----------|----------------|
| Revenue Growth (ex-acquisitions) | >5% strong; <0% patent cliff | Organic business trajectory |
| Key Product Concentration | Top 3 drugs <50% healthier; single drug >30% risky | Revenue concentration |
| Patent Cliff Exposure (3-5 years) | <30% of revenue manageable; >40% concerning | Revenue sustainability |
| Volume vs Price Growth | Track separately | Pricing power vs. demand |

### Pipeline & R&D Metrics (ALL REQUIRED)

| Metric | Benchmark | Why It Matters |
|--------|-----------|----------------|
| R&D as % of Revenue | 15-25% typical; <10% underinvestment | Future growth investment |
| Pipeline Value (risk-adjusted) | Growing relative to current revenue | Future revenue replacement |
| Phase 3 Assets | 5-10+ for diversified large pharma | Near-term launch visibility |
| Approval Success Rate | Track vs. industry average | R&D productivity |

### Profitability Metrics (ALL REQUIRED)

| Metric | Benchmark | Why It Matters |
|--------|-----------|----------------|
| Gross Margin | 75-85% for specialty pharma; 60-75% generics | Pricing power |
| Operating Margin | >30% excellent; 25-30% good; <25% concerning | Scale efficiency |
| R&D Yield | Revenue per R&D $ over time | Research productivity |
| FCF Conversion | >80% strong | Cash generation |

## Required Quirks (Section 3)

### 1. R&D Expense Treatment (US GAAP)

- **What:** US GAAP expenses all R&D immediately; IFRS capitalizes development costs
- **Distortion:** GAAP earnings lower during heavy R&D investment; comparability issues across standards
- **Adjustment:** Add back R&D to calculate "pre-R&D" operating income for cross-company comparisons

### 2. Milestone and Royalty Revenue

- **What:** Licensing deals generate milestone payments (event-driven) and royalties (sales-based)
- **Distortion:** Milestone revenue lumpy and one-time; can materially inflate/deflate quarterly earnings
- **Adjustment:** Separate milestone revenue from product revenue; model royalties as recurring

### 3. Collaboration Accounting

- **What:** Joint ventures and licensing deals create complex revenue sharing arrangements
- **Distortion:** Revenue may be gross or net depending on principal vs. agent determination
- **Adjustment:** Understand collaboration economics; track net profit share not gross revenue

### 4. Inventory Write-downs and Pre-Launch Stock

- **What:** Companies build inventory before approval; write down if approval fails
- **Distortion:** Large inventory write-offs on failed approvals; pre-launch builds inflate working capital
- **Adjustment:** Track inventory relative to near-term approvals; flag unusual builds

## Valuation Guidance (Section 8)

### Primary Valuation Method

**Sum-of-Parts: DCF on Current Products + Risk-Adjusted Pipeline NPV**

| Business Quality | Justified P/E | Rationale |
|------------------|---------------|-----------|
| Strong growth, deep pipeline, no near cliff | 18-25x | Premium for visibility |
| Moderate growth, adequate pipeline | 12-18x | Fair value |
| Patent cliff exposure, thin pipeline | 8-12x | Discount for risk |

### Secondary Methods

- **EV/EBITDA:** 12-18x for quality; useful for M&A context
- **Pipeline NPV Addition:** Add risk-adjusted pipeline to current business value
- **Revenue Multiple:** Only with margin and growth context

### Methods to AVOID

| Method | Why Inappropriate |
|--------|-------------------|
| P/E without patent cliff adjustment | Earnings about to decline |
| Revenue multiples across different margins | Specialty vs. generics differ |
| Book value | Intangibles dominate |
| Simple DCF without pipeline | Ignores major value driver |

### DCF Considerations

- **Patent cliff modeling:** Explicit revenue decline when patents expire
- **Pipeline probability:** Phase 1: 10%, Phase 2: 25%, Phase 3: 50%, Filed: 80%
- **Launch curves:** Peak sales at year 5-8 post-launch typical
- **Biosimilar/generic erosion:** Model post-LOE revenue decay

## Peer Selection (Section 7)

### Match On

| Factor | Why It Matters |
|--------|----------------|
| Therapeutic focus (oncology, immunology, rare disease) | Different economics |
| Business model (innovative vs. specialty vs. generic) | Different margins and growth |
| Pipeline stage and depth | R&D risk profile |
| Patent cliff timing | Revenue trajectory |
| Geographic sales mix | Pricing environment |

### Flag as False Comparables

| Company Type | Why Not Comparable |
|--------------|-------------------|
| Generic manufacturers | Completely different model |
| Biotech (pre-revenue) | No established commercial ops |
| Drug distributors | Different value chain position |
| Medical devices | Different regulatory and sales model |
| CROs/CDMOs | Service vs. product company |

## Red Flags to Document

| Red Flag | What It Signals |
|----------|-----------------|
| Major patent cliff <5 years with thin pipeline | Revenue drop ahead |
| R&D declining as % of revenue | Future underinvestment |
| Single product >40% of revenue | Concentration risk |
| Phase 3 failures | Pipeline setback |
| Pricing pressure on key drugs | Revenue at risk |
| Inventory builds without near-term launches | Demand or approval concerns |

---

## Green Flags to Document

| Green Flag | What It Signals |
|------------|-----------------|
| Pipeline value exceeds current market cap | Optionality |
| Multiple Phase 3 assets in different areas | Diversified pipeline |
| Strong launch trajectory on new drugs | Execution capability |
| Pricing power maintained | Brand strength |
| R&D productivity improving | Innovation engine working |
