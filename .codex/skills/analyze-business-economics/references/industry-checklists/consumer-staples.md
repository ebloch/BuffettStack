## Required Metrics (Section 4)

### Revenue Growth Metrics (ALL REQUIRED)

| Metric | Benchmark | Why It Matters |
|--------|-----------|----------------|
| Organic Revenue Growth | 4-6% strong; 2-4% adequate; <2% concerning | Core business momentum |
| Volume Growth | 1-3% healthy; negative for 2+ years concerning | True demand indicator |
| Price/Mix Contribution | 2-4% typical; >5% demand destruction risk | Pricing power but limit |
| Developed vs. Emerging Market Growth | Emerging >developed expected | Growth runway indicator |

### Profitability Metrics (ALL REQUIRED)

| Metric | Benchmark | Why It Matters |
|--------|-----------|----------------|
| Gross Margin | 55-65% for premium brands; 40-55% for food | Brand pricing power |
| Operating Margin | 25-35% for concentrate model; 15-25% for full production | Business model efficiency |
| EBIT Margin Trend | Stable or expanding | Pricing vs. cost balance |
| FCF Conversion (FCF/NI) | >90% strong; <80% concerning | Earnings quality |

### Return Metrics (ALL REQUIRED)

| Metric | Benchmark | Why It Matters |
|--------|-----------|----------------|
| ROIC | >15% excellent; 10-15% good; <10% concerning | Capital efficiency |
| Return on Brand Investment | Marketing spend effectiveness | Brand building ROI |
| Dividend Payout Ratio | 50-70% typical; >80% may limit reinvestment | Capital allocation |

## Required Quirks (Section 3)

### 1. Bottler vs Concentrate Accounting

- **What:** Concentrate model (KO) books ~$45B revenue; full production (PEP) books ~$90B
- **Distortion:** Revenue numbers not comparable; margins look completely different
- **Adjustment:** Compare gross profit dollars, not revenue; understand business model first

### 2. Refranchising/Acquisition Accounting

- **What:** Bottler acquisitions and refranchising create one-time gains/restructuring charges
- **Distortion:** Earnings inflated by transaction gains; comparability destroyed
- **Adjustment:** Track "comparable operating income" excluding structural changes

### 3. Commodity Hedging in OCI

- **What:** Companies hedge commodities (sugar, aluminum, corn); mark-to-market in OCI
- **Distortion:** OCI swings; hedge gains/losses flow through COGS when realized
- **Adjustment:** Analyze input cost trends separately; understand hedging strategy and duration

### 4. Channel Stuffing Risk

- **What:** Quarter-end promotions can pull forward sales into current period
- **Distortion:** Revenue recognized in Q1 may be Q2 consumption; inventory builds
- **Adjustment:** Track inventory at distributors; compare shipments to consumption/scan data

## Valuation Guidance (Section 8)

### Primary Valuation Method

**DCF on Owner's Earnings (bond-like characteristics)**

| Growth + Quality | Justified P/E | Rationale |
|------------------|---------------|-----------|
| Global leader, 4%+ organic growth | 25-30x | Premium for durability |
| Regional leader, 2-4% growth | 20-25x | Solid franchise |
| Struggling growth, volume decline | 15-20x | Turnaround needed |

### Secondary Methods

- **EV/EBITDA:** 18-25x for premium; useful for comparisons
- **Dividend Yield:** 2.5-3.5% for quality; bond proxy
- **FCF Yield:** 4-5% typical for stable

### Methods to AVOID

| Method | Why Inappropriate |
|--------|-------------------|
| Revenue Multiples | Concentrate vs. bottler distortion |
| Raw Margin Comparisons | Business model differences |
| P/E without hedging normalization | Commodity swings distort |
| Same metrics across all CPG | Model differences matter |

### DCF Considerations

- **Volume vs. pricing:** Model separately; volume more valuable
- **Commodity exposure:** Hedging strategy affects near-term margins
- **Currency exposure:** Major global exposure
- **Reinvestment rate:** Marketing spend as investment

### Owner's Earnings Adjustments

```
Net Income                                    $XX,XXXM (FYxxxx)
+ D&A                                          $X,XXXM [per 10-K]
- Maintenance CapEx                           ($X,XXXM) [EST: 50-60% of total]
- Refranchising Gains                         ($XXXM) [one-time]
+ Restructuring Charges                        $XXXM [if non-recurring]
+/- Hedge Gain/Loss Normalization               $XXXM [if material]
= Owner's Earnings                           ~$XX,XXXM

Note: Stable FCF generation is hallmark of quality staples
```

## Peer Selection (Section 7)

### Match On

| Factor | Why It Matters |
|--------|----------------|
| Business model (concentrate vs. full production) | Completely different economics |
| Category (beverages vs. food vs. HPC) | Different margins and dynamics |
| Geographic mix | Emerging vs. developed exposure |
| Brand portfolio (premium vs. value) | Pricing power differences |
| Channel mix (retail vs. foodservice) | Different customer dynamics |

### Flag as False Comparables

| Company Type | Why Not Comparable |
|--------------|-------------------|
| Bottlers vs. concentrate companies | Different model |
| Grocery retailers | Customer, not peer |
| Restaurants/QSR | Different consumption model |
| Alcohol companies | Different regulatory and distribution |
| Private label manufacturers | No brand value |

## Red Flags to Document

| Red Flag | What It Signals |
|----------|-----------------|
| Volume decline for 3+ quarters | Demand erosion |
| Price increases >5% without volume loss | Unsustainable elasticity |
| Gross margin compression from commodities | Pricing power limits |
| Market share losses to private label | Brand value declining |
| Emerging market growth stalling | Long-term runway concerns |
| Excessive promotion depth | Brand equity erosion |

---

## Green Flags to Document

| Green Flag | What It Signals |
|------------|-----------------|
| Organic revenue growth 4%+ with positive volume | Healthy demand |
| Market share gains in key categories | Competitive strength |
| Gross margin expansion despite inflation | Pricing power |
| Successful premiumization | Trading up in portfolio |
| Emerging market revenue >30% growing | Long runway |
