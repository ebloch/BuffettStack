# Shareholder Letter — Event Checklist

**Event Characteristics:**
- Typically annual (with annual report or annual meeting)
- Written by CEO (sometimes CEO + Chairman)
- Philosophical and reflective in tone
- Long-term perspective
- Often reveals management's mental models

**Primary Focus:** Philosophy, vision, and long-term thinking

---

## Mandatory Extraction

### Philosophy & Values

- [ ] **Core beliefs** — what management believes about business/markets
- [ ] **Mental models** — frameworks they use for decisions
- [ ] **Long-term vision** — 10+ year perspective
- [ ] **Company culture** — how they describe it
- [ ] **Stakeholder philosophy** — shareholders vs. other stakeholders
- [ ] **Capital allocation philosophy** — how they think about deploying capital

### Retrospective

- [ ] **Year in review** — key accomplishments
- [ ] **Mistakes acknowledged** — what went wrong
- [ ] **Lessons learned** — insights from experience
- [ ] **Prior predictions** — did they come true?
- [ ] **Changes in thinking** — evolution of views

### Forward Vision

- [ ] **Industry outlook** — where they see the sector going
- [ ] **Company positioning** — how they fit in that future
- [ ] **Strategic priorities** — what matters most
- [ ] **Risks acknowledged** — what could go wrong
- [ ] **Opportunities identified** — what excites them

### Management Quality Signals

- [ ] **Candor level** — honest about problems?
- [ ] **Ownership thinking** — treat it like their own money?
- [ ] **Long-term orientation** — patient or short-term focused?
- [ ] **Humility** — acknowledge uncertainty and limits?
- [ ] **Clarity of thought** — can they explain simply?

### Quotable Passages

Extract verbatim quotes that reveal:
- [ ] Management character
- [ ] Strategic thinking
- [ ] Values and priorities
- [ ] Risk awareness
- [ ] Competitive philosophy

---

## Red Flag Triggers

Watch for and flag if present:

- [ ] Blame external factors for all problems
- [ ] No mention of mistakes or challenges
- [ ] Vague platitudes without substance
- [ ] Excessive focus on stock price
- [ ] Promotional tone (selling the stock)
- [ ] Contradictions with prior letters
- [ ] Avoiding discussion of failed initiatives
- [ ] Excessive self-congratulation
- [ ] Complex language masking simple problems

---

## Quality Signals

Positive signs to note:

- [ ] **Candor** — discusses both successes and failures
- [ ] **Specificity** — concrete examples, not just generalities
- [ ] **Consistency** — aligned with prior communications
- [ ] **Humility** — acknowledges uncertainty
- [ ] **Owner mindset** — thinks like a long-term owner
- [ ] **Clear thinking** — explains complex ideas simply
- [ ] **Accountability** — addresses prior commitments

---

## Berkshire-Style Analysis

For exceptional shareholder letters (Buffett, Singleton, etc.), also extract:

- [ ] **Teaching moments** — business/investing lessons
- [ ] **Industry insights** — unique perspective on sector
- [ ] **Competitive dynamics** — how they see the game
- [ ] **Capital allocation logic** — reasoning behind decisions
- [ ] **Risk management** — how they think about downside
- [ ] **Quotable wisdom** — memorable formulations

4. What are they NOT saying that they should be?
5. Would you want to partner with this management team?
